# Skill: Opinion

An opinion piece argues a clear position. The reader should finish knowing exactly what you believe and why, even if they disagree. Hedging and balance for its own sake weakens the piece — acknowledge counterarguments, then rebut or qualify them.

---

## Required sections (in order)

1. **Thesis** — one or two sentences. The position the article will defend. State it plainly in the opening paragraph.
2. **Why this matters now** — the context or trigger that makes this argument timely or relevant.
3. **The case** — three to five supporting arguments, each with specific evidence: data, personal experience, observed patterns, or documented incidents. No vague generalisations.
4. **The strongest counterargument** — steelman the opposing view. State it as fairly as you can.
5. **The rebuttal** — why the counterargument does not invalidate the thesis, or under what conditions the thesis still holds.
6. **Takeaway** — what the reader should do or think differently as a result. Concrete and actionable.

---

## Style rules

- First person ("I", "we" if speaking as a team or community). Own the opinion.
- Specific over general: "In the three services I migrated last year" beats "In my experience."
- Short paragraphs. One idea per paragraph.
- No weasel words ("sort of", "kind of", "arguably") unless immediately followed by a qualification.
- Headers are optional in the body — use them if the piece is long enough to need navigation, not as decoration.

---

## What to avoid

- A thesis that is not actually contestable ("clean code is good").
- Listing facts without arguing a position.
- A takeaway that is more hedging ("think carefully about your use case").
- Quoting or citing without explaining what the evidence proves.
